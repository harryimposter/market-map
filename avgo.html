The Quarter in Brief

Broadcom grew fast but fell short of a very high bar. Revenue rose 48% year-over-year to $22.19B, yet that slightly missed Wall Street's ~$22.27B estimate. Earnings per share beat ($2.44 vs $2.40), and AI semiconductor revenue jumped 143% to $10.8B. The problem wasn't growth — it was that management left its AI and 2027 sales forecasts unchanged when investors had priced in a raise. With the stock having rallied into the print, that "good but not better" outcome triggered a sharp sell-off.

What Missed & Why the Stock Is Down

Three things. First, revenue came in just below estimates — a small miss, but a miss after the stock had run up into the report. Second, gross margin slipped to 77.1%, down 230 basis points year-over-year and below where the Street modeled it. (Operating margin actually rose to 67.3%, up 200 bps, because costs stayed flat — so profitability per dollar of sales held up; it was the headline gross-margin line that disappointed.) Third, and most important, CEO Hock Tan reiterated the 2027 AI forecast rather than raising it. Investors had pre-paid for an upgrade that didn't come. With the market also nervous about rising interest rates, the combination sent shares down more than 13% after hours. The business is still growing rapidly — this is a reset of expectations, not a sign of decline.

Why the Gross Margin Slipped

This is a "good problem." Broadcom's AI semiconductor business is growing far faster (+143%) than its higher-margin software (VMware) and legacy non-AI chips. AI chips carry lower gross margins than software, so as they become a bigger slice of the mix, the blended gross margin falls — even though margins within each product line are stable. Management was explicit that the decline is mix-driven, not a sign of pricing pressure or rising costs. In plain terms: the fastest-growing, lower-margin part of the company is becoming the majority, which mechanically pulls the average down while overall profit dollars keep climbing.

Why They Didn't Raise the 2027 Guidance

Broadcom first set its "$100B+ in 2027 AI revenue" target back in March 2026, and on this call Tan reiterated it rather than lifting it — the single biggest source of disappointment, because expectations had crept above $100B. A few reasons behind holding the line: (1) it's still early — that target is more than a year out and depends on large, lumpy custom-chip programs with a handful of hyperscaler customers, so management has little incentive to over-promise; (2) Tan prefers to guide conservatively and let results beat, a long-standing pattern; and (3) the forecast is anchored to booked programs, and he signaled the underlying capacity is in fact growing — pointing to shipping more than 10 gigawatts of AI chips in 2027, a slight increase from prior estimates. So the headline number stayed flat, but the supporting detail quietly improved. The market wanted the headline raise; it got reiteration plus a hint of upside instead.

Post-Market Action

Shares fell more than 13% in extended trading immediately following the release, giving back the gains made heading into the print. The move reflects positioning and a high valuation meeting an in-line (rather than blowout) result — not a deterioration in fundamentals.

Bullish Thesis — Three Reasons to Look Through the Dip
01

AI Growth Is Real and Huge

AI chip revenue grew 143% year-over-year to $10.8B — a genuine boom, not a forecast. Broadcom designs the custom silicon that powers AI data centers for the largest cloud companies, putting it at the center of the single biggest spending wave in tech. Even without a forecast raise, the underlying demand is accelerating.

02

Guidance Still Points Higher

Management guided next quarter's revenue to ~$29.4B, up 84% year-over-year — well above what analysts expected. A company "disappointing" while still guiding to 84% growth tells you how high the bar has become. The trajectory remains strongly upward; the market simply wanted even more.

03

A Wide, Profitable Moat

Beyond AI chips, Broadcom owns VMware, a sticky enterprise-software business that throws off recurring, high-margin cash (company-wide EBITDA margins near 68%). That mix of cutting-edge silicon and dependable software is hard for rivals to copy and helps fund continued investment — a durable franchise, not a one-product story.

Near-Term Catalysts to Watch

A few things could move the stock from here. (1) Analyst price-target revisions over the next several days — if the Street largely holds its targets despite the unchanged forecast, that can stabilize sentiment after the drop. (2) New customer or AI-bookings disclosures — any named hyperscaler commitment or backlog update would directly address the "why no raise?" concern. (3) The June Federal Reserve meeting — with markets now pricing high odds of a rate hike this year, the rate path is a major swing factor for expensive growth stocks like Broadcom. (4) Peer read-through — results and commentary from Nvidia, Marvell, and the big cloud spenders will signal whether AI demand is still broadening or starting to cool.
