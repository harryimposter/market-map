2 · The Wrap

Layer 1 — the one driver. Oil. Brent fell from $116 on 1 May to $91 on 29 May, a 21% collapse, on the Iran ceasefire framework that promises to de-mine and reopen the Strait of Hormuz after three months of paralysis. That single move explains 60–70% of everything else: the US 10-year fell from a 16-month high of 4.70% on 20 May to 4.44%, the dollar index slipped to a two-week low near 99, and the S&P printed its ninth straight up week into 7,580. A 17% energy decline is a disinflation impulse that does the Fed's easing for it. The whole complex is trading that impulse.

Layer 2 — the counter-intuitive hook. The market is long the peace before the peace exists. The MoU is a 60-day clock that has not started: Tehran rejected the Friday terms, and the same weekend CNN-tracked Pentagon line had US forces "ready to resume combat." The risk premium has been removed from crude faster than the risk has been removed from the Gulf. The disinflation everyone is buying is a derivative of a document nobody has signed. If it fails, the energy relief reverses and the Fed that is priced to cut is priced to hold — or hike.

Layer 3 — three buckets, alpha in the gaps. A) Real economy: core PCE 3.3%, unemployment 4.3%, growth intact. The oil drop has not touched a CPI print yet — it is a forward impulse, not a realised one. B) Priced: nine weeks of equity gains, IG option-adjusted spreads at 80bp and high yield at 285bp (both rich), VIX 15. The tape has priced the soft landing and the oil relief together. C) Consensus: 67.8% of the FedWatch distribution says no cuts in 2026, yet bonds rallied. The gap is the trade — the rates market is front-running a cut the survey insists will not arrive, and it is right to, because a sustained Brent in the $80s rolls headline CPI lower into the autumn whether the survey likes it or not.

The regime is a single-factor tape wearing a diversified costume. One barrel is setting the correlation matrix, and the breaks are where it has already changed the driver.

  • Energy equity decoupled from the energy barrel. Brent −17% on the month while GE Vernova is +54% YTD, Quanta +75%, and Bloom Energy +109% in a single month. The break is total: "energy" stopped meaning crude and started meaning electrons. The bid is AI-electricity demand, not the oil price — the driver under the sector flipped from commodity beta to data-center capex.
  • Stocks and bonds rose together. Equities to a ninth up week while the 10-year rallied 26bp off its high. Positive return correlation, because both assets share one input — oil — and both are pricing the disinflation it implies. That is not diversification; it is two expressions of the same trade, and it unwinds in one direction if Brent reclaims $95.
  • Gold broke from real yields. Spot gold at $4,523 with the 30-year at 4.98% and the dollar barely soft. Gold is supposed to hate a 5% long bond; it is ignoring it. The driver is debasement and geopolitical-hedge demand, not the real-rate discount — gold is the one asset pricing both tails of the MoU at once.

  • AUD shrugged off the commodity rout. EURAUD broke to new lows — Aussie strength — even as the whole commodity complex fell. The break: AUD is trading the rate differential and the electrification-metals bid, not its old terms-of-trade link to a falling energy and bulk-commodity tape.
