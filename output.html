The Wrap

The market has made a bet. Broadcom reports tomorrow after the close, and the consensus has decided this is a confirmation event — a formality that rubber-stamps the AI capex cycle for another quarter. It isn't. At 41 times forward earnings, AVGO is pricing six more consecutive AI revenue beats. An in-line print is not neutral. An in-line print at 41x is the hidden bear case, and nobody is positioned for it.

The anatomy of today's market is this: the entire AI trade — from NVDA's Nemotron 3 Ultra keynote yesterday, to AMD's Lisa Su at Computex Day 2 today, to the custom ASIC buildout at Microsoft, Google, Meta, and TikTok — runs through a single proof point that prints tomorrow night. If AVGO guides Q3 AI revenue above $11.5 billion, the cycle is confirmed and every multiple is justified. If it guides in-line or guides conservatively on hyperscaler ASIC pull-through, the 140% YoY growth rate decelerates and funds that paid 41x for perpetual acceleration have to reprice simultaneously. There is no middle path at these valuations.

L1 — The driver. This is not a macro day. It is a single-stock binary sitting inside a macro portfolio. The June 3 AVGO print is the most important earnings event of the quarter for the AI trade — not because AVGO is the biggest AI name, but because its custom ASIC revenue is the cleanest measure of what hyperscalers are actually committing to in capex, as opposed to what they are announcing. Jensen Huang announces. AVGO invoices.

L2 — Counter-intuitive hook. The consensus is positioned for a beat. Six consecutive quarters of AI revenue beats has trained the market to expect the seventh. But the pain trade is not a miss — it's guidance that is technically a beat on the quarter but conservative on the forward look. "AI revenue $10.9B vs $10.7B expected, Q3 guided at $11.0B" is a beat on the headline and a re-rate on the stock. The market is not positioned for careful guidance from a company trading at 41x.

L3 — The gap. What's priced: perpetual AI capex acceleration, six-plus beats, 140% YoY AI revenue growth sustained through 2027. What's possible: Microsoft, Meta, and Google have all given cautious capex commentary on the margin. AVGO's four hyperscaler ASIC clients each have their own deployment timeline. If one pulls forward a refresh cycle and another delays, the blended AI revenue growth rate looks deceptively smooth in the aggregate but the forward book is thinner than the headline suggests. That gap — between invoice-level reality and street-level extrapolation — is what tomorrow's print resolves.

Burry tell. AVGO has four hyperscaler ASIC customers. Concentration is the structural risk that 41x doesn't price. One customer — say, Meta pauses its Llama-5 training run — and AVGO's revenue book is not four customers wide. It's three. The street models a diversified AI capex cycle. The ASIC business is a two-to-three client business wearing a four-client coat. The tell will not appear in the headline revenue. It will appear in the Q&A, in the tone around customer naming, and in whether management gives a specific revenue commitment for Q4 or hedges with "strong pipeline."

Pozsar mechanic. AVGO's AI revenue is paid in advance — hyperscalers commit to multi-year ASIC development programs and pay NRE (non-recurring engineering) fees upfront. The revenue recognition is lumpy but the cash flow is front-loaded. This means AVGO's reported AI revenue is a lagging indicator of actual hyperscaler commitment, not a leading one. By the time the revenue prints, the real capex decision was made 6–12 months earlier. Tomorrow's print tells you what Google and Meta decided in Q4 2025. The actual current commitment level lives in the backlog and the Q&A, not the headline.

Papic constraint. All four hyperscaler ASIC customers are US-domiciled. The US chip export control regime is the political constraint behind AVGO's growth ceiling. The current administration has been expanding export controls on advanced AI hardware to China. AVGO's custom ASIC business does not have China exposure — but the regulatory precedent is being set in a direction that constrains the total addressable market for the next generation of ASIC programs. If the administration tightens further in June, the constraint lands in Q4 2026 guidance, not tomorrow's print.
