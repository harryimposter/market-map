The wrap

The market is buying a disinflation that exists only on a desk in Washington — the Brent collapse everyone is treating as easing is a derivative of a memorandum Trump has not signed, and the second-order effect nobody is pricing is what happens to a 22x multiple when an unsigned document is the only thing holding crude down.

Decompose the headline. The S&P at 7,580 and a ninth straight up week reads as broad strength until you find the anatomy: the gains belong to the AI-electrification complex — GE Vernova +54% YTD, Quanta +75%, Bloom Energy +109% in a month, Lumentum +121%, Applied Materials +67% — while Nvidia itself is up 12% and the median S&P name is flat. The tape is not nine weeks of breadth. It is one capex theme migrating down its own supply chain, financed by a 26bp bond rally that the front end says is a rate cut. The headline says melt-up. The anatomy says a single trade wearing a diversified costume. So what: own the migration, not the index. Who's wrong: anyone treating this as a healthy broadening. The trade: long the picks-and-shovels layer, short the barrel it is decoupling from.

L1 — the one driver. Oil. Brent fell from $116 on 1 May to $91, a 21% collapse, on the Iran ceasefire framework that promises to de-mine and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. That single move dragged the 10-year from a 4.70% high to 4.44%, pushed DXY to a two-week low, and floated equities to records. A 17% energy decline is a disinflation impulse doing the Fed's easing for it. The regime: borrowed disinflation, melt-up tape — relief bought on credit, with the bill dated to the MoU.

L2 — the hook. Consensus expected a Gulf war to keep an inflation premium in crude into the summer. Instead it got a ceasefire and a 60-day clock — and the clock has not started, because Tehran rejected Trump's Friday terms on Hormuz, the nuclear stockpile and frozen assets, and US forces are staged to resume combat. The risk premium left crude faster than the risk left the Gulf.

L3 — three buckets. A) Real economy: core PCE 3.3%, unemployment 4.3%, growth intact — the oil drop has not reached a single CPI print. B) Priced: nine up weeks, IG spreads 80bp and high yield 285bp, VIX 15 — the soft landing and the oil relief, banked together. C) Consensus: 67.8% of the FedWatch distribution says no cuts in 2026, yet bonds rallied. The gap between B and C is the alpha — the rates market is front-running a cut the survey insists will not come, and a sustained Brent in the $80s makes it right by autumn.

L4 — Bull / Base / Bear. Mapped to the tiles right. The base case is a grind, not a melt-up or a crash: the MoU drags through its window and nobody gets paid on conviction.

L5 — priced vs not-priced. Fully priced: the soft landing, the AI-capex supercycle, the oil relief. Mispriced the wrong way: the MoU's fragility, a payrolls miss read as bad-news-bad, and the carry unwind if the BoJ hikes off a 30-year-high 0.75% into a 6–3 split that already wanted 1%.

The tell nobody is looking at. Gold at $4,523 with the 30-year at 4.98% is not a geopolitical hedge — it is the market pricing fiscal dominance it will not name out loud. An asset that should hate a 5% long bond is making records against it. In six months that decoupling resolves into the story: the bid is not Iran, it is the structural debasement that a Fed pinned by 3.3% core and a Treasury pinned by supply cannot stop. That is the Burry tell — own the gold, ignore the rationalisation.

The plumbing. Trace the bond rally to the flow, not the narrative: with the reverse-repo facility drained to a residual and reserves still ample, there is no funding stress absorbing the move — the sub-80 MOVE confirms the disinflation rally is real positioning, not a liquidity accident. The one pipe to watch is the AI-capex investment-grade supply wave; data-center and power financing is the wall of issuance that cheapens the front of credit before any macro print says it should.

The constraint. Trump owns the oil price now. The political constraint is that he needs the MoU to hold to keep gasoline falling into an election-sensitive autumn, which caps how hard Tehran can be pushed — and Tehran knows it. The ceasefire holds not because the framework is sound but because both sides need the optics. That is the trade: the peace is more durable than the headlines and more fragile than the price.
